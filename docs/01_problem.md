# 1. The clinical problem

> **BTH is a research prototype, not a medical device.**

## Arm function after stroke

Weakness of one side of the body (hemiparesis) is common after stroke, and the arm is often slow to recover.
Recovery is most likely in the first weeks and months, and depends strongly on how severe the initial
weakness is.

- In a prospective study of patients whose arm was flaccid in the first week after stroke, about 38% regained
  some dexterity by six months, and 11.6% reached complete functional recovery
  (Kwakkel G, Kollen BJ, van der Grond J, Prevo AJH. Probability of regaining dexterity in the flaccid upper
  limb: impact of severity of paresis and time since onset in acute stroke. *Stroke*. 2003;34(9):2181-2186).

This figure is for severe initial weakness. Patients with milder weakness recover more often. The point for
BTH is narrower: a large group of patients lives with a partly recovered arm, and rehabilitation for them is
long and effortful.

## Mood after stroke

- A systematic review and meta-analysis of 61 observational studies (25,488 people) estimated that about
  31% of stroke survivors have depression (pooled frequency across time points, 95% CI 28% to 35%;
  lower, about 23% to 25%, in studies one to five years after stroke)
  (Hackett ML, Pickles K. Part I: frequency of depression after stroke: an updated systematic review and
  meta-analysis of observational studies. *Int J Stroke*. 2014;9(8):1017-1025. doi:10.1111/ijs.12357).

Depression and rehabilitation interact: therapy is effortful, and a patient who is overwhelmed or
disengaged gets less out of it.

## What BTH targets

BTH does not treat depression and does not diagnose it. It targets one measurable piece of the problem:
the patient's physiological response to the effort of therapy, measured during the session, so that
assistance can be adapted to the individual patient and a clinician can see how the patient responded.

## What is not claimed

- No claim that BTH improves motor recovery, mood, or any clinical outcome. No patient has used it.
- No claim that autonomic signals measure mood or depression. They reflect arousal and autonomic load,
  which are related but not the same.
